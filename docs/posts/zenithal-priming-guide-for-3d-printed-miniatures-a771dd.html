Zenithal Priming Guide for 3D Printed Miniatures

August 01, 2026

Prime your 3D printed miniatures in matte black spray. Let the base coat dry completely. Hold a white spray primer forty-five degrees above the model. Spray short bursts from top to bottom. This builds instant natural shadows in every recess.

Thin your acrylic paints with water before applying base colors. Transparent glazes work best for fast miniature painting. Let the light preshading do the heavy lifting for cloth and armor definition. Dark areas stay dark without slathering heavy washes. Your D&D miniatures gain immediate depth on the table.

Catch raised edges with a subtle drybrush. Pick a pale tone for hard armor plates and sharp weapon edges. Wipe off almost all paint onto a paper towel before touching the model. Flick the bristles gently across high points. Tabletop wargaming miniatures need high contrast to read clearly from three feet away.

Finish with a light coat of spray matte varnish. Gloss finishes reflect room light and hide your shadow work. A clean matte seal protects resin from handling during game night. Your free STL miniatures are ready for the tabletop.
